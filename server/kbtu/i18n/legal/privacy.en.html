Privacy Policy

Revision of 22 November 2026

KBTU App is an independent student app available in Telegram, in a browser and from the home screen. Kazakhstan law governs data processing. The Russian text prevails if translations differ.

The app is not an official service of JSC “Kazakh-British Technical University” and does not act on its behalf.

1. Operator and consent

The database owner and operator is Nurmatov Sukhrab Rustamzhanovich, the creator of KBTU App. For data questions and requests, contact @Sowers on Telegram.

We collect only the data needed for the stated purposes and on a lawful basis. Consent is limited to the data, purposes, period and transfer conditions shown, including public disclosure and transfers abroad. When you give consent, we record its version and time. Event teams, Secret Santa, People of the app, appeals, questions to a person and AI help with appeals use their own consent forms. Declining optional processing affects the selected feature.

On first opening, a guest record is created with Telegram data and opening times, before general consent. Data about people without accounts may also come from lists, groups and the team’s records of contributions to the app. The operator needs a lawful basis for processing, including collecting and storing a Telegram ID before an account is created. If data is provided by another party, that party also needs a lawful basis. Group membership or continued use alone does not replace consent where required.

2. Sign-in, verification and profile

  • Telegram. ID, first and last names, username, language, photo link and copy, and permission for the bot to message you. We use these for sign-in, your profile and contact.
  • Sign-in outside Telegram. When you use Telegram Login in a browser or home-screen app, we set a session cookie with a random token to keep you signed in. The server links the token’s digest and its creation and last-use times to your account. We delete the session on sign-out, after 60 days of inactivity, when the account is deleted, or 90 days after sign-in (14 days for staff and admins). You can end all browser sessions at once in Settings. A short-lived digest of the Telegram response prevents reuse.
  • KBTU Microsoft account. To verify you, we receive account ID, display name, first and last names, login, email, account type, company or category, creation date and country of use. You enter the password at Microsoft. Sign-in data and a one-time secret are stored for the return from the browser. Authorised employees use a staff identifier in place of a student number. We keep the authorisation and the time it was granted.
  • Profile. First and last names, student number, year, school, phone, city, nickname, photo, bio, award showcase and appearance settings. Student verification status is visible. Your school may determine event access. Older accounts may contain data from the former portal, including programme and timetable.
  • Lists. Clubs, the Department of Youth Policy (DMP) and organisers provide lists for membership, attendance checks and awards. Lists may include full name, contact details, student number, school, year, city, position, department and participation. Matches are used to link lists to accounts. A city and phone from a list may fill in your profile.

3. Study, connections, events and clubs

  • Timetable. From the page you send the bot, we take your name, student number and class details: subject, code, type, day, time, room, teacher and titles in different languages. The source file is not kept. A verified match may update your number, with the old and new values recorded. You can see your full timetable. Section 6 explains KRI’s context.
  • Connections. Friends and requests, follows, saved events, invitations and who invited you. A link can record who invited you before sign-in. They see the total and the award.
  • Events and QR. Registration, waiting list, ticket, QR, arrivals and departures, who recorded the scan, answers, invitations and refusals, volunteer applications, ratings and reviews. For attendance checks, an organiser can upload a list of expected people and see its fields and attendance. A matching email at Microsoft verification may fill in the student number. Older named guest records contain full names and times. A qualifying recent record can be linked at verification if the name matches uniquely.
  • Guest lists. For some events, the organising club sells tickets outside the app and gives us buyers’ student numbers, full names and, for 18+ venues, whether each buyer is 18 or older. We use the names to match tickets. For buyers who have yet to join the app, we keep their student number, the sheet’s 18+ mark and a one-way code of the surname until their ticket is linked at sign-in or until 24 hours after the event ends, whichever comes first.
  • Age checks at the door. At events marked 18+, door staff check an identity document and record only “18 or older” or “under 18”. We keep no date of birth or document details. The mark is visible to that event’s door staff. If the venue requires it, the bot uses the mark to message guests under 18 asking them to leave the venue. We delete the mark 24 hours after the event ends.
  • Event teams. We keep the name, link, roster, joining order, captain and matching preference. Signing up for a paid event via a link can save the team choice until a receipt is submitted. If you have yet to consent, signing up for an available place opens one prompt. Confirming sign-up there records consent to show your name, nickname and photo to the team and to assignment by the organiser or app. Consent covers team events until withdrawn. Joining the waiting list alone does not give consent. See section 8 for visibility and section 11 for withdrawal.
  • Secret Santa. At sign-up, we show a separate consent form and keep a record of participation and the wishlist. After the draw we store who gives to whom. Your Santa can see your name, nickname, photo, profile link and wishlist. You can see the same details for your recipient and your own wishlist. Before the draw, the organiser can see the names and photos of participants who have signed up and club members who have yet to sign up. After the draw, the game’s management view shows only the participant count. Wishlists and pairings are hidden in that view. Losing a place or deleting an account changes the pairings. After the draw, in games of four or more participants, you and your Santa can exchange messages in the game; your Santa stays unnamed to you. Only the two of you can see the messages. If any participant leaves the game, the chat closes for everyone and the messages are deleted. If either of you reports the chat, it is closed and the app’s staff can see it and both participants in order to handle the report. Messages are deleted 3 days after gift day and with the account; a reported chat is kept for 30 days after gift day and, while the report is unhandled, for no more than 90 days after it.
  • Recruitment and membership. The recruitment team receives the applicant’s profile and contacts, answers, departments and priorities, application source, past participation and applications, number of friends in the club and account creation date. We keep scores, notes, interviews, attendance and the club’s decision. Membership management uses roles, positions, departments, tags, dates, probation, history, activity, removal requests and bans with reasons.
  • Club event reports. For the club’s internal records, we keep a team member’s attendance mark for each event (present, late, absent or excused) and an optional comment of up to 300 characters on that mark. We record who filled in the report or wrote the comment and when, and who last edited it and when. Only people in that club with the Event reports permission can read and edit the report. Your profile shows your own marks and their comments. People with that permission can request a CSV of the club’s team log with names, events, dates, marks and comments. The bot sends it to the requester’s private Telegram chat. Files already sent stay in the recipients’ chats until deleted there. App staff have no access through their staff role. We ask for separate consent (evr2) inside the feature before you can be marked or open a report. Your marks and their comments are kept while you have an account, including after you leave the club. If you withdraw consent or delete your account, we delete your marks and their comments and remove links to you as an author or editor of reports and comments. Other club records remain.
  • Finding people and study partners. We keep skills, visibility and notification settings, requests, contact offers and outcomes, blocks and reports. Skill search shows people who enable it. A subject card shows profiles, years, schools and notes of people who list themselves for study partners. A block by either person hides them from each other. Telegram contact is shown after both agree.

Telegram membership checks. To manage membership in linked chats, we check IDs and membership status for eligible verified students with current consent. Manual removals and bans are taken into account. Checks pause if we cannot confirm status or if many changes look mistaken.

/all. For mentions in the same group, the bot and service account use the group ID, Telegram IDs and last-seen times from messages, joins or member lists, including people without an app account. We check membership. Authorised club members or group admins can use the command, which can repeat the caller’s short text.

4. Payments, support and volunteer hours

Tickets. The organiser receives the Kaspi transfer. The app keeps the receipt image, ticket link, amount, number, parties’ names, payment time, error flags and the organiser’s decision with reviewer and time. The receipt is read on the server. A hash, receipt number and replacement time help detect reuse. When the organiser rejects your payment receipt, they can choose a reason from a fixed list, and the bot tells you that reason.

Support and awards. A Stars gift record contains amount, payment ID and day, without a link to the payer’s account. Fuel is linked to its recipient and the award does not show the amount. For personal awards, we keep recipient, date and reason. A club card may show the year and title of a KBTU Awards category it won. Additions and removals are logged.

Volunteer ledger. The club provides data on a lawful basis. We keep list entries, linked profile, hours, absences, shifts, missed checkout records and edit history. New shifts come from scans, including automatic shifts for door workers. Older manual entries are retained. Shift duration is capped when counting hours. A delegated door holder sees names and times for their own check-in session and can record missed departures. Presidents and vice-presidents can read, correct and grant access. Authorised readers can see the ledger, and volunteers can see their own entries. Ledger access currently checks consent to its section as part of general consent, with an exception for manually admitted staff and admins. This does not prove consent to importing data or recording hours.

5. Publications, photos and anonymity

Gallery and albums. We keep photos, profile attribution, author, event link and time. Visibility depends on the album and event. You can attach a photo after check-in, and the team can add photos under album rules. Capture time from album uploads is used to sort those photos. Other metadata is removed. Authors can delete their publications. You can report a photo, including when you are in it and object.

Gallery place labels. If you agree when adding a Gallery photo, the app reads its stored location on your device and shares only one label with us and people who can see the photo: main building, building 2, dorm or off campus. The coordinates stay on your device, and the uploaded photo contains no location metadata. You can upload without a label. We keep your choice and when you made it.

Event document. The organiser uploads a PDF and the server converts it to images. The title and page images are kept for the event card. The original PDF is not kept. Information on the pages remains visible, and the images can be opened by direct link.

Public forum. The bot publishes approved posts and photos to the public channel. People can read, copy and forward them. A signed post shows a short name, with nickname and photo also shown in the app. Anonymous posts use a pseudonym. AI checks text. A moderator checks disputed cases and anonymous posts with photos. Photos are re-encoded without source metadata. We keep text, photos, author link, time and check results.

Anonymous replies. After a separate check, the bot publishes text with a mask and the post’s pseudonym. We keep the text, target comment number, status, time, check results and message number. The author link is through the post. Authors can delete replies and moderators can take them down. Reports record the reporter and time.

Limits of anonymity. Readers, moderators and other admins cannot see the author of an anonymous post or reply. Only the creator can identify the author from the record in exceptional cases involving a threat, a violation of law or a lawful request from a competent authority. Access is logged. Disclosure requires a lawful basis and is limited to what is necessary. Text, photos or circumstances may identify the author independently.

Discussions and sharing. In-app comments and reactions are stored. Telegram discussions use Telegram profiles and text. The feed shows a summary of participants and reaction counts. Section 7 explains how individual comments are processed. A live post’s share card quotes its opening without the signature. Telegram’s sharing search shows your own signed posts. A sent card can remain after removal.

Channels and directory. From linked public channels we copy text, photos, view and reaction counts, and the channel’s own translations from comments. Videos and files open in Telegram. Removing the source takes down the copy. Turning off syncing leaves earlier copies. A qualifying announcement may go to AI for an event draft with a source link and translations, which an authorised person checks. A directory suggestion stores the link and an internal link to the suggester for replies.

People of the app. To record contributions to the app, the team keeps the Telegram ID, the type of contribution (an idea, a bug discovery or code), a short description in the team’s own words without a name, and the release date. The Telegram ID can be recorded before an account is created. All app users can see entries for contributions included in the app. Without consent, each entry appears separately as “Student”, with no profile details or visible link between entries by the same person. Your name, nickname, photo and profile link appear alongside these and future entries about your contributions only after you consent in the app. If the bot can message you, it may invite you to respond once per version of the form. You respond in the app. We keep your response, the form version and the time. After you withdraw consent in Settings → Privacy, the entries are again shown separately as “Student”. The app’s creator is listed without separate consent.

Feedback and helping the app. Anonymous feedback about the app is stored with no link to an account: only the text and the day it was sent. Do not put personal data in it. The app’s administrator reads the feedback, and it is deleted after 90 days. An offer to help the app is signed: with your account we keep what you offer, an optional line and the time. The app’s creator sees your name and Telegram username to contact you; you consent in the form itself. An offer is deleted after 180 days or together with your account. The bot tells the creator only that something arrived, never the text.

6. Assistant, appeals and map

KRI. To answer, AI receives the question, recent turns, school, year, estimated intake year, today’s and tomorrow’s classes (subject, type, time and room), public event and campus information and, when opened from a card, its public content. Profile name and contact fields are omitted. Data you type stays in the text. In the live setup the app server searches university documents. Relevant excerpts and sources go into the request for an answer.

Ordinary questions and answers are currently kept out of server conversation history. We store account and time to limit assistant and event-fill requests. To fill a form, AI receives the announcement and existing fields. Topics of unanswered questions and ratings are kept without account links for statistics. After a negative rating you can separately send admins one question and answer without an account link. Some class details are removed from the answer, but the text may still identify someone. Displays of crisis help are counted in aggregate.

Appeals and questions to a person. Student Government and the help desk have separate consent and reading permissions. We keep text, topic, status, answer and times, with the account link stored separately. You and appointed staff at the selected desk can read the text. A signed submission shows a short name without profile or contacts. For anonymous submissions, only the creator can access the author under section 5. Actions are logged without text. AI helps with drafts on request and with separate consent to transfer abroad. Student Government and the creator see appeal counts by topic, with rare topics grouped together. Public results summarise outcomes without quotes or author links.

Map. To fix a problem we keep the description, account, map version, floor, selected point and time, then the resolution time. You select the point. The people responsible for the map see the notes.

Notifications. The bot usually sends a template with a link. Before Secret Santa sign-up closes, the bot may remind club members who have yet to sign up about the deadline. It may also remind participants about the event on the day before gifts are exchanged. Public posts, /all, text returned to its author and the title of your own find-people request are sent in their respective features. Personal notifications can be configured in the app.

7. Reports, protection and statistics

A report records the target, reason, note, reporter, time and decision. Moderators can remove or restore content. Reviewers can see reporter details. AI checks find-people texts, study-partner notes and new skills without an attached profile. Qualifying reports can temporarily hide content pending a decision.

Telegram /report. We keep the text, including edits, link, author and reporter details and decision. AI receives the text. Qualifying student reports are counted by time since Microsoft verification, rather than account creation. Automatic removal based on those reports also requires an AI finding of a violation. For comments without verification, the bot may remove the text, briefly keep it to return to its author, and record a warning or restriction. We take confirmed report abuse into account.

Word filter. In a linked group the bot checks new and edited text and captions locally. Matching messages are deleted. The log keeps IDs, the dictionary match, time and warning. A temporary posting restriction applies when the warning threshold is reached. Authorised club members can see the log.

Protection and counters. Technical logs may include time, address and errors. We track account-linked openings, views of unfamiliar profiles, staff actions and profile edits. Aggregate usage counters are saved without links to people or sessions. IDs are used temporarily in memory to prevent inflated counts.

Automated checks, list matching and team assignment can affect access, publication and team rosters. Automated processing that changes rights or legitimate interests requires consent or a legal basis in legislation. You can ask how the processing works and what it means for you, and object for human review via @Sowers. We review objections and report the outcome within three working days. Section 11 sets out your rights.

8. Who can see data

  • Profile. Students see short name, nickname, photo, card number, year, school, bio, attendance count, available awards and a visible city. Friends and clubmates also see memberships and participation history. Friends see upcoming events accessible to them that you plan to attend, according to your setting. Fuel visibility follows your showcase choice. Your follows, saves and full timetable are not shown through your profile.
  • Club. A club page shows the roster according to its access setting and a club contact. Club teams receive member details according to their roles. DMP can access the club register with full name, student number, year, school, role, joining date and activity, and leaders’ contacts. Recruitment and ledger data go to the people described in sections 3 and 4.
  • Event. The organiser’s authorised team can see full names, registrations, check-in records, answers and receipts according to its permissions. The club members who run the event can open your Telegram username to write to you about your place or ticket while you hold a place at that event and for 7 days after it ends. Openings are recorded in the action log. If you have no username, the organiser can ask the bot to send you a message with a button to contact them on Telegram. The message contains no text written by the organiser. The club’s leaders and other members with permission to manage its events can export the participant list, as can app administrators with access to that event. The list contains participants’ Telegram usernames. Exports also contain study and contact details, including phone only if the event requested it. Co-hosts see short names and registration and attendance details, without receipts or answers. Door helpers see check-in data according to their access. List exports can be sent as Telegram files and recipients retain copies. Clubs receive reviews without the author’s name. We save when you dismiss the rating prompt for an event. Club and school pages show only an overall average from public events with at least three ratings after voting closes.
  • Teams. Members see their own roster with names, nicknames and photos. A signed-in student with the link sees the event, team, captain and participant count. Authorised organisers see rosters and people without teams. For Santa, see section 3.
  • Calendar. Anyone with the personal link can see events you registered for or hold a place at, with times, venues, clubs and links, including cancelled events. The feed contains no classes. You can replace the link.

The operator and other recipients’ access is limited by their permissions and the purpose. A competent authority may receive necessary information where there is a lawful basis. We do not sell personal data. The app has no advertising and we do not share data for advertising.

9. External recipients and cross-border transfers

The current register of recipients lists purposes, data and countries or regions. Routing depends on the feature, configuration and scope of consent.

  • Telegram (team in the UAE, international infrastructure). Account data for sign-in, IDs and statuses for groups, messages, posts, photos and files for delivery and display, and payment data for Stars.
  • Microsoft (USA, international infrastructure). Sign-in request and KBTU account data to verify students and employees.
  • Cloudflare (USA, international network). IP and traffic for service delivery and protection. For recovery, we send encrypted backups to Cloudflare R2. They include the database, payment receipts, media files and files used to reapply deletions of account data. Cloudflare R2 stores these backups outside Kazakhstan.
  • Munito AI France (France; EU or global host), GroqCloud (USA; international infrastructure), Cloudflare Workers AI (USA; international network) and Google (Gemini API, Google AI Studio; USA; international infrastructure). Depending on the feature and consent, they receive the KRI context described above, text to check or explain, appeal drafts with separate consent, announcements and event fields to fill forms, event, club and channel text to translate, and public chat details for classification. A request may go to several authorised recipients. Google may receive the context described above to answer a KRI assistant question. On its free tier, Google may use request content to improve its products and models. Do not include personal details in your question.

Transfers outside Kazakhstan require lawful conditions and must stay within the consent obtained where it is required. Transfer consent does not override legal requirements, including for storage. Retention by recipients depends on their processing terms.

10. Retention

Kazakhstan law requires personal data to be stored in a database or digital object located in Kazakhstan. The app’s production server and primary database are on university premises in Kazakhstan. For recovery, the server keeps the 14 most recent nightly database backups. Backup cleanup removes nightly copies older than 15 days and pre-migration copies older than 14 days. Copies for Cloudflare R2 are encrypted on the server before upload. The R2 retention policy protects copies from deletion for 29 days and schedules deletion 30 days after upload. The conditions in section 9 apply to this cross-border transfer. Data leaves the live database when deletion is carried out. Completed deletions are applied on restoration.

  • Profile, connections, timetable and membership are kept to operate those features. Unfinished sign-ins are kept for completion. Ticket, team, QR, payment, volunteer and Santa data support running events, recordkeeping and resolving discrepancies. Application answers are kept for selection and objections.
  • Appeals, feedback, map notes, reports and decisions are kept for review, fixes and reconsideration. /all, membership-check and filter data support group operation and resolving disputes. Logs and counters support security and statistics.
  • The bot queue is kept for delivery and checking the outcome. Cleanup takes account of the scheduled send time and whether delivery succeeded or failed. Data is deleted once its purpose is met unless the law requires otherwise.

Some images and videos are accessible by direct link. Caches, forwards, screenshots and recipients’ exports may remain after deletion on our side.

11. Rights and account deletion

Through @Sowers you can ask about your data and obtain its categories, sources, purposes, methods, recipients and retention periods. You can request correction backed by evidence, blocking or destruction of unlawfully processed data, deletion, anonymisation or restriction under law, and withdraw consent. People without accounts have these rights too. You can go to the competent authority or court and seek protection and compensation under law.

We handle requests within legal deadlines, including one working day in cases set by law. On withdrawal, we stop processing under that consent within 15 working days or give a reasoned refusal if the law requires continued processing or storage. Statutory requests are handled separately from ordinary account deletion.

Settings → Delete account. The screen shows the time to cancel and photo options. Ordinary deletion waits if you have a ticket or hold for an upcoming event with a submitted receipt that has not been rejected. Data needed to fulfil a paid obligation is kept to the extent necessary.

  • Deletion removes your profile, timetable, connections, permissions, team and Santa records, linked appeals, responses about being shown in People of the app, receipts and linked ledger records. Gallery photos are deleted and there is a choice for older album photos.
  • Live forum posts, replies and undeleted comments may remain without an account link. Text and photos may still contain personal details. Delete an item separately or contact the operator.
  • Contribution entries in People of the app remain labelled “Student”. The Telegram ID is removed from those records.
  • Restrictions and record hashes may be retained for protection and to prevent reimport. Map notes and aggregate results can remain without an account link. Gifts and KRI feedback without an account link cannot be located by account. Records for replaying deletion and remaining copies follow section 10.

You can withdraw team consent on the team card. Withdrawal currently removes you from teams for events yet to start and keeps event registration. For other consents, use feature settings or contact the operator.

12. Protection and age

Connections are encrypted and access is checked against permissions. We notify the competent authority of a personal data security breach within one working day of discovery. Affected people are notified as required by law. Where the law requires a representative’s consent, it must be obtained before the relevant processing.

13. Policy changes

The current revision is available in the app. We give notice of material changes before applying them. Where a new purpose, data category or transfer falls outside existing consent, we explain it first and obtain consent in the relevant feature. Publishing a revision or continued use alone does not give that consent.

14. Public forms

KBTU App can host public forms (questionnaires), for example for research by the university or its partners. Anyone with the link can open a form, including people without an account.

We keep only the answers you type or select and the time you submit them. We do not store names, accounts, IP addresses or cookies with the answers. Your answers are anonymous unless you include details that could identify you. Please leave out those details.

The app’s team and the form organiser (for example, researchers) can see the answers. They receive the answers in aggregate or table form for the stated purpose. We delete the answers when they are no longer needed for that purpose, no later than one year after the form closes.

By submitting the form, you consent to the processing of your answers for that form. Participation is voluntary. You can skip any question unless it is marked as required.

15. Research in the app

Research surveys in the Forum are for verified students, with one response per account. Their public link directs people to KBTU App and does not accept answers. Earlier public answers remain anonymous; we do not try to identify their authors.

By default, answers are signed. We store the answers, account identifier, submission day and separate consent version. The app creator can export the answers with the student's name and account identifier for the purpose stated in the survey. The sheet shows who sees the name. By submitting, the student consents to this use separately from account consent. Participation is voluntary.

You can turn on “Answer anonymously” before submitting. The app creator receives the answers without the name or account identifier. The answer uses a random identifier and records no submission time. To prevent repeat answers, a separate record keeps the survey and account identifiers, with no reference to an answer. Leave identifying details out of your answers.

We delete signed answers and participation records with the account. Anonymous answers remain. We delete research answers and participation records when they are no longer needed, within one year after the survey closes.

Proposing research. A verified student can send a title, goal, up to ten questions and details of who runs the study. With the proposal we store the account link, separate consent version, time and review status. App administrators and moderators see the proposal, name and Telegram username for contact; only the app creator approves it. Approval does not automatically publish a survey. The proposal is deleted after 180 days or with the account.
